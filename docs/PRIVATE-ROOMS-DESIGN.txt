1. THREAT MODEL (DECIDED)
Assets: room keys k_R^e; plaintext of sealed entries/atoms; membership integrity (who can read
epoch e). Parties:
 - OPERATOR / HOST: stores the keys cell, signs views, relays every intent. Primary model:
   honest-but-curious (runs the protocol, reads everything it stores). Secondary, named: ACTIVE
   (serves stale/forged views, withholds, reorders). It is also the only validator on devnet, so
   "rooted in chain state" == "the operator's word": chain-sourced authority adds nothing against
   it today. Authority therefore comes from KEYS PINNED OUT OF BAND, not from served state.
 - OTHER MEMBERS: honest about their own key; may be kicked; a kicked member keeps the past.
 - STALE RECIPIENT: whoever holds a member's superseded X25519 secret (old device, leaked
   KEY.enc-ring, a member about to be kicked). A wrap of an already-used key addressed to it is a
   disclosure the moment the ciphertext leaves the founder's custody — CAS refusal later does not
   un-send it.
Goals: G1 the operator never learns a room key or plaintext (passive). G2 the operator cannot
make a writer seal under a key it chose (no forged epoch). G3 no client seals under an epoch older
than one it has authenticated (monotone). G4 no ciphertext of an already-used key goes to a
recipient whose currency was not re-confirmed after the release was durably bound. G5 an atom
edit cannot be silently rolled back to an earlier ciphertext at the same address.
Non-goals / residuals (said out loud): (R1) FREEZE: an active operator can keep a member that has
NOT yet seen epoch e+1 on epoch e by withholding (incl. the pre-kick view) — single-server fork
consistency at best; needs out-of-band or multi-host gossip. (R2) First sight of an atom: a reader
with no history accepts any internally consistent ciphertext the operator chooses to show at the
current revision (G5 holds for readers with history, and for the create->edit edge for all
readers). (R3) Metadata: cell ids, writers, heights, epochs, members' public keys, sizes in 64-byte
buckets. (R4) Founder signing-key rotation: CLOSED by sec 7 (a cross-signed transition chain; no re-pin).
(R5) PQ: CLOSED for wraps and escrow by sec 6 (hybrid X25519 + ML-KEM-768); signatures stay
Ed25519 (authentication is not harvestable). A member's SIGNING key rotation still needs the founder
to re-pin it out of band (sec 7, last paragraph). (R6) Every member reads every wrap (sec 9, J-PRIV-1).
Everything here: DEVNET QUALITY; PRIVACY NOT AUDITED.

2. KEY LINEAGE (DECIDED)
 - FOUNDER PIN. The founder is the only writer of wraps (keys law, Kernel/PrivateRoomKeys.lean
   keysLaw_refuses_nonfounder_wrap). Each client pins (room id, keys cell, founder Ed25519 key)
   in ROOT/private/room-founder-ROOM.json: the founder at `found`; an invitee when it runs
   `room-key --op recipient-record --founder-key HEX` (the same exchange that carries its signed
   148-byte declaration back to the founder). No pin => sync refuses. Re-pinning is explicit
   (`--op pin-founder --replace true`).
   FIRST-CONTACT TRUST, said plainly: the pin is TRUST ON FIRST USE through whatever channel carried
   the key. On a single-validator devnet, if that channel is the node (a chat line, a served view),
   it is TOFU-through-the-operator: an active operator could hand the newcomer its own key and run
   the room for it. The out-of-band option is the FINGERPRINT (fingerprint() = first 10 bytes of
   cSHAKE over the key, grouped): `found` prints the founder's; every pin prints the pinned one; the
   member compares them with the founder directly (in person, a channel the node does not carry).
 - MEMBER PIN. The founder pins each member's signing key from the out-of-band 148-byte signed
   declaration (`invite`, or `--op pin-member`). A record in the keys cell authorizes a wrap only if
   it verifies under that pin (operator-substituted records refused). A member whose signing key
   changed must re-declare out of band.
 - EPOCH CERTIFICATES (192 bytes, existing format): signed by the pinned founder key; parent =
   identity of the previous certificate. Every wrap carries its epoch's certificate and a founder
   delivery signature over (epoch, gen, member, X25519 key, wrap, grant, certificate identity).
   The founder signs each delivery individually: the wrap set of an epoch grows by invites, so a
   per-delivery signature is the right granularity (withholding one member's wrap = DoS on that
   member, which it sees).
 - CHAIN RULE: certificates form one chain from genesis (epoch 0, zero parent); each certificate's
   epoch is STRICTLY GREATER than its parent's (not necessarily +1: an epoch burned by a dead draft
   is skipped). Conflicting certificates for one epoch refuse.
 - CLIENT MONOTONICITY: ROOT/private/epoch-head-ROOM.json retains (epoch, identity). A served chain
   whose head is lower, or equal with a different identity, refuses; so does a ring key above the
   head (kept from HEAD). Forgetting keys never lowers the head.
 - DEAD DRAFTS: at most one live release draft per room. Starting a new release operation marks
   every older undisclosed draft of the room DEAD (dead.json, terminal): its key is never installed
   in the sealing ring and its ciphertexts are never sent. A rotation always draws a fresh key at
   max(head, every epoch named by any release record) + 1, so a burned epoch is never reused, and
   once a later epoch lands the chain itself excludes the dead one for every reader.

3. ENVELOPE BINDING (READ + DECIDED)
READ: at HEAD a room-key envelope is never re-sealed at an existing address. Fresh private text in
documents is refused unless it goes through protected-document audiences
(protected_document::reject_fresh_legacy runs before private::seal_content, workspace.rs:2840), so
seal_content's create/edit sealing branches are unreachable; its only live case is a tombstone that
retains the old ciphertext. The one live room-key seal is a stream append at the signed view's
nextSeq (workspace.rs:3209), and a stream position holds exactly one entry forever. So the
"same-address rollback" of R2-1 #3 has no live producer in private.rs.
DECIDED: (a) the envelope's address IS its revision (a stream position is written once), already
bound in commit and AAD; no new field (a field that binds nothing new is a no-op); (b) delete the unreachable document create/edit sealing in seal_content
(a second sealing shape that agrees today is one that disagrees later); legacy room-key document
atoms are read-only + tombstone. (c) The SAME defect lived on in the successor format: protected_atoms.rs bound [1 ‖ atom id] only,
so an edited protected-document atom re-served with its earlier fragment opened. FIXED (same lane):
binding v2 = 2 ‖ atom ‖ seq ‖ prev revision, carried in the fragment header and in the fragment
operation every open recomputes. Reader checks: seq = 0 iff revision = createdAt (struck lines
exempt), and a retained per-atom version (protected-documents/atom-versions-OBJECT.json: seq,
revision, ciphertext digest) that refuses a lower seq, the same seq with other bytes, or a next seq
whose prev is not the revision last seen. Residual R2 stands: a reader with no history, shown a
consistent stale RECORD (revision rolled back too), cannot tell. v1 bindings refuse to load.

4. PRE-DISCLOSURE ORDERING (DECIDED) — per release operation, persistent under
ROOT/private/room-releases/OPERATION/:
  DRAFTED  draft.json: exact signed deliveries (ciphertexts), certificate, key encrypted apart
           from the sealing ring. Nothing has left custody. Older drafts of the room -> DEAD.
  BOUND    turn 1 (proposal ID-bind) creates one RELEASE atom per delivery in the keys cell, id =
           ((2^30 + 1 + e) << 96) | gen << 64 | member, payload = founder-signed statement
           (room, keys, epoch, certificate identity, member, gen, digest of the recipient's signed
           record, grant, delivery commitment = cSHAKE of the complete signed wrap). No ciphertext.
  APPLIED  turn 1 source-confirmed (accepted_outcome), THEN a fresh signed view of the keys cell is
           read back and must show every release atom byte-exact, every recipient record still
           equal to the bound digest and verifying under its member pin, the head unchanged
           (invite: head = bound epoch; rotation: head = parent), and each recipient still in the
           Host's `who`. Any difference -> DEAD (terminal), nothing disclosed.
  DISCLOSED turn 2 (proposal ID-wraps) creates the wraps whose commitments equal the bound ones;
           on confirmation the rotation key enters the sealing ring. UNKNOWN outcomes recover the
           same attempt (same bytes); they never re-author.
Readers accept a wrap only if a founder-signed release atom at its release address commits to its
exact bytes. A burned address (dead draft whose release atom applied) is never reused: re-invite
needs a new member record generation or a rotation.

5. WHAT "PRIVACY NOT AUDITED" MUST SAY (now in FRIENDS.md, docs/PRIVATE-CELL.md, the template README)
Label: "devnet quality; privacy not audited; founder-key pin is trust-on-first-use via the operator
unless verified out of band." Then, in words: the node stores the room's messages encrypted and
cannot read them while it follows the protocol; it CAN see who is in the room, who wrote when, and
sizes to 64 bytes; a misbehaving node can hide a key change from a member until that member has seen
it once (after that the client refuses to go back), so a member who has not synced since a kick can
be kept on the old key; compare the founder fingerprint directly; a hosted member puts the room key
on the box; not post-quantum.

6. PQ: HYBRID WRAPS (READ + DECIDED; BUILT, lane s9-rooms 2026-10-04)
Before: wraps were ECIES = ephemeral X25519 + cSHAKE256 KEK + XChaCha20-Poly1305 (v1): a recorded
wrap was harvest-now-decrypt-later exposed. Now (wrap v3, record v3, escrow v2, ring v2):
 - A member's encryption identity is a PAIR derived from the one signing seed: X25519 =
   cSHAKE256("DREGG.CLIENT.ENC/v1"; seed); ML-KEM-768 = FIPS 203 seed d||z (64 bytes) =
   cSHAKE256-XOF("DREGG.CLIENT.KEM-SEED/v1"; seed), expanded by AWS-LC's seeded key generation. A
   seed alone recovers every key, as before. The 32-byte KEY ID (cSHAKE256 over both public halves)
   names a pair in wraps, releases and selectors.
 - The signed member record v3 (1333 bytes) = key epoch ‖ X25519 (32) ‖ ML-KEM-768 encapsulation key
   (1184) ‖ room ‖ keys cell ‖ CUSTODY byte (sec 8) ‖ signing key ‖ signature; the signature covers
   both keys. A wrap atom carries the recipient's key id (not the 1216-byte key): payload 1532 bytes.
 - A wrap = ephemeral X25519 (32) ‖ ML-KEM-768 ciphertext (1088) ‖ nonce (24) ‖ box (48).
   KEK = cSHAKE256(customization "DREGG.PRIVATE-WRAP.KEK/x25519+ml-kem-768/v3";
        ss_x25519 ‖ ss_ml-kem ‖ transcript), every input length-prefixed, transcript = frame ‖ room ‖
   epoch ‖ member ‖ ephemeral key ‖ KEM ciphertext ‖ recipient X25519 key ‖ recipient ML-KEM key. It is
   also the AEAD's associated data. One hash of BOTH secrets and every public value: secure if EITHER
   primitive survives; a ciphertext or key substituted in either half changes the KEK.
 - The ML-KEM implementation is aws-lc-rs 1.18.1 (ML_KEM_768), already in the lock and used by
   pq_mailbox.rs; no new crate. The one new edge is a direct dependency on aws-lc-sys 0.45.0 (the same
   locked version) for AWS-LC's seeded key generation, which aws-lc-rs does not expose and aws-lc-sys's
   portable bindings omit: it is declared extern against the version-stamped symbol, so a bump fails
   to LINK, loudly. Both the combiner (independent cSHAKE256) and the seeded key (independent pure-Python
   FIPS 203) are pinned by known-answer tests.
 - NO DUAL PATH. v1/v2 records, v2 wraps (the whole keys cell refuses), v1 keyrings, v2 declarations
   refuse by name; the old wrap code is deleted. Every private room is re-founded.
 - Not MLS: one founder, small rooms, explicit rotation; MLS adds tree state without a threat this room has.
 - Residual (said out loud): signatures are Ed25519, so a quantum adversary can forge FUTURE founder
   authority (certificates, releases, transitions) once it can break the pinned key; it cannot read
   recorded wraps. Sizes: a member record is 1.3 KB and a wrap 1.5 KB of JSON-hex ~3 KB, so a full
   64-delivery release draft is ~240 KB against the 256 KiB retained-record bound (tested).

7. FOUNDER-KEY TRANSITION (DECIDED + BUILT; Rust only, no Lean change: the keys law sees ids, not payloads)
Problem: the founder is a pinned Ed25519 key. `rotate-key` replaces the signing key, so after a
rotation the founder's client refused to sign (its key was not the pin) and no member would verify
what the new key signed: the founder was locked out of its own room until every member re-pinned.
 - WHO MAY ROTATE: only the current tip of the room's founder-key chain. A TRANSITION record is signed
   by the OLD key (this key succeeds me, for epochs strictly above E) and by the NEW key (I hold the
   secret: possession proof; an unheld or hostile key cannot be installed) over the same statement
   "DREGG/PRIVATE-ROOM-FOUNDER-TRANSITION/v1" ‖ room ‖ keys cell ‖ index ‖ E ‖ identity of the epoch
   certificate at E ‖ old key ‖ new key. Atom id ((2^31 + 1 + index) << 96): the founder-only region
   of the keys law, above every release; payload 248 bytes.
 - WHAT MEMBERS VERIFY: the transitions form ONE chain (index 0,1,2.., each old key = the previous
   new key, E never decreasing, each naming the certificate the cell shows at E, no key reused). The
   client's pin may be ANY key on the chain: an earlier key authenticates it forward, a LATER key
   authenticates it backward (every link needs that later key's own signature, so an operator without
   the secret cannot extend or fork it). The certificate of epoch e must be signed by the key in effect
   at e (the number of transitions with E < e selects it); deliveries and releases of epoch e by that
   key or any later one (a founder may invite into an old epoch after rotating). The client retains the
   number of transitions it authenticated and the tip (epoch-head-ROOM.json): a served cell with fewer,
   or the same number ending elsewhere, REFUSES (a hidden or forked hand-over).
 - WHAT REFUSES: a transition not signed by both keys, naming another room/keys cell, a certificate
   the cell does not show or another than it shows, a gap in indices, a fork, a return to a retired
   key, a pin on no link; an epoch certificate signed by a retired key for an epoch it no longer owns;
   and the founder's own client signing with anything but the tip (`require_tip`).
 - ORDER (the two-phase rule, applied): `room-key --op transition --next-key KEY.next` is run BEFORE
   the Host rotation, while the old key is still the Host-valid signer of the write: it BINDS the
   record, reads the keys cell back and requires the chain to end at the new key. `rotate-key` then
   runs `founder_rotation_gate` and REFUSES a founder whose next key is not yet the tip of every
   private room it founded (fail closed: an unreadable room refuses too). Nothing secret is disclosed
   by a transition, so there is no draft/dead-draft state; the write is idempotent under its proposal id.
 - LIMIT (said plainly): the cell cannot say WHEN a delivery was signed, so a delivery of an epoch the
   retired key owned still verifies under it. What stops a retired key signing is the Host (it revokes
   the old key at rotation) and the tip guard of the founder's own client. A COMPROMISED founder key
   is a compromised room (the attacker can hand it over too); the answer is a new room.
 - NOT COVERED: a MEMBER's signing-key rotation. The founder pins each member's signing key from its
   signed declaration; a rotated member re-declares out of band (`room-key --op pin-member --replace true`).
   The same chain could be applied per member; not built (no threat beyond convenience).
 - Verbs: `room transition ID ROOM NEXTFILE` (shell), `mini workspace --action room-key --op transition`.

8. HOSTED CUSTODY AND THE BRIDGE (BUILT)
 - The member's SIGNED key record carries a custody byte (1 = my signing key is a file on a shared box;
   `keygen --hosted`, what the hosted shell runs, leaves KEY.hosted and `room register` / the signed
   declaration read it). An inviter refuses a hosted-declared member without --i-know, whether or not the
   operator listed it in /etc/mini/hosted-subjects (either signal refuses). The byte is under the
   member's signature: it cannot be laundered to "own machine".
 - ONE invite path: the shell's `room invite`, `chat invite` and `summon` run the same room-key
   invocation (roomkey::invite_flags). `chat invite` runs `invite-check` (every refusal, nothing
   written) BEFORE it publishes the grant and births the stream, so a refused invitee never holds a grant.
 - The Discord mirror: a private room's opened text is structurally unable to reach the channel.
   `tail --json` stamps a boolean `private` on the header and every entry; the mirror's `PublicFeed`
   refuses a feed that says private, says nothing, or mixes the two; `outbound` accepts only the entries it
   yields. No setting lifts it. (A mirror that is a hosted MEMBER of a private room is the invite rule above.)

9. J-PRIV-1: PER-WRAP GRANTS (DESIGNED; kernel work AUTHORED, NOT COMPILED; see the lane STATUS)
Today every member's room grant reads the whole keys cell, so a member sees every wrap (ciphertext it
cannot open: with hybrid wraps nothing is gained from copying them, but a leaked read capability shows
the whole membership/epoch structure and every ciphertext to a harvester). Wanted: a member reads only
its own wraps, releases and record. What it needs, and where each part stands:
 (a) KERNEL: a field of a content cell that names atoms by their low 64 bits. `CellField` has `body`
     and `annotations` for content; add `atomsOf (low : Nat)` covered by `body`
     (`NamedBy fields (atomsOf m) := atomsOf m in fields or body in fields`; `SetNarrows` by covering,
     not subset), `contentField` maps an atoms-namespace address to `atomsOf (atomLow id)`, the capability
     codec/Host JSON name it `atoms:M`. Low-half granularity (not per (epoch, member) atom) is deliberate:
     a member's future wraps carry new epochs and generations but the same low half, so ONE grant covers
     every epoch without a re-delegation per rotation. AUTHORED (uncompiled) in commit "lean(J-PRIV-1)".
 (b) CLIENT, once (a) lands: `room invite` delegates observe with fields=[atoms:M] (+ the certificate
     region, below) instead of the whole cell. The existing flat hiding root already shows a narrowed
     reader sealed leaves for uncovered atoms (count and positions, not values); the per-address tree view
     of K-FIELDS-OBSERVE would hide those too and is NOT on main.
 (c) FOUND, and the real cost: `verify_lineage` needs the WHOLE cell: the epoch-certificate chain is
     checked from genesis over all wraps' embedded certificates, and every release is cross-checked. A
     narrowed member sees only its own wraps, so it cannot check the chain. Fix (client, with (b)): give
     each epoch certificate and each founder-key transition its own atom in a region every member's
     field covers (low half 0), and let a narrowed reader check (1) the certificate/transition chain
     from those, and (2) only ITS OWN wraps against ITS OWN releases. This changes the wire (certificates
     leave the wrap payload) and the order of the release state machine; it is Rust-side but pointless to
     land before (a). Filed with the next concrete step in cv.
Until (a)-(c) land: every member reads every wrap; the label says so (R6).
