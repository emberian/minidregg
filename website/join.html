Join

Mini is early. The way in is the hosted shell on the public node, and the guide for it is FRIENDS.md. Read it first: it says, section by section, what the node has and what only main has.

Getting an account

Accounts are given by hand. You make an SSH key for this, send its public half and the session name you want, and are enrolled; then you reach a mini> prompt over SSH. The steps, and how to reach ember, are in FRIENDS.md. Paying to enrol is designed but not deployed.

What you can do on the public node today

  • Make a key and a workspace, create resources, propose and submit changes, and read them back.
  • Delegate a narrower grant to another member, publish it, export and import references, and revoke a grant.
  • Set a resource's law as a JSON predicate, including a permanent lock.
  • Retry an attempt and get the same receipt; list your history.
  • Documents: new, show, append, edit, link and backlinks; and boards.

This set passed the shell journey, J0 to J8.

What you cannot do there yet

  • Rooms, chat, private rooms, jobs and sealed markets.
  • Key rotation (a committed next key), and proxy mode, which keeps your key on your own machine.
  • The librarian (Hermes), the Discord entrance, and paying to enrol.
  • The newer document verbs and can, why, inspect and law checking.

Before you rely on anything

It is a devnet. When the data model or the kernel wire changes, the store is re-genesised: your SSH key and signing key stay, and everything you made is gone. Nothing has an uptime promise.

Your key is on the box. In the hosted shell your signing key is made on the operator's machine. The operator, and every process that runs as the box's service account, including other members' sessions, can read it. Keep nothing precious behind a hosted key.

Contributing

Start with the repository's README, AGENTS.md and the developer guide. Nothing in Mini has users or a compatibility obligation, so a format change is a rebuild, and an old shape should refuse to load rather than be reinterpreted. Say what a change breaks.
